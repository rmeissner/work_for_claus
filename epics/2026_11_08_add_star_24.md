## Architecture Decision

### Four planes through one common axis

The four parts stand in four planes that all contain the star's **vertical axis**, rotated 45° apart (0°, 45°, 90°, 135°). Every star is turned so that a **notch** (not a point) lies on the axis at the top and at the bottom. Then no two parts share a point: 4 × 6 = 24 distinct points, in three rings of 8 (upper, equator, lower).

All parts meet only along the vertical axis, so every slot runs along it. The axis inside the stars (from the bottom notch to the top notch, `2 × INNER_RADIUS` long) is split into **four segments**, one per part. Along the axis, each part keeps material only in its own segment and has slots everywhere else:

```
 axis      part A      part B      part C      part D
 top notch   ██          ┊┊          ┊┊          ┊┊
             ██          ┊┊          ┊┊          ┊┊       ██ material on the axis
   r/2  ───  ┊┊  ─────── ██  ─────── ┊┊  ─────── ┊┊       ┊┊ slot
             ┊┊          ██          ┊┊          ┊┊
   0    ───  ┊┊  ─────── ┊┊  ─────── ██  ─────── ┊┊
             ┊┊          ┊┊          ██          ┊┊
  -r/2  ───  ┊┊  ─────── ┊┊  ─────── ┊┊  ─────── ██
 bottom      ┊┊          ┊┊          ┊┊          ██
```

- Part A has one slot from the bottom notch, part D one slot from the top notch, parts B and C one from each side. Every slot is open to the outline and no two slots touch, so each part stays one piece.
- **Assembly:** hold part A, then push B, C and D up onto it from below, one after another, each turned 45° further, until it stops. A part only ever slides along the axis, and its own segment never passes another part's segment, so this works without bending anything (checked in the tests by sweeping each part in).

### Toolkit change

`layout_on_plate` currently places parts in one row. Four ~90 mm stars in a row are ~380 mm wide, more than the 220 mm plate. It gets an optional `columns` argument (default: all parts in one row, so the star ornament's STL does not change) and fills rows of `columns` parts, `gap` apart, centered on the origin.

### Alternatives Considered

- **Tetrahedral arrangement** (the four planes of a cuboctahedron, each pair crossing on a different line): looks a bit more uniform, but each part then needs slots along three different directions. A part can only slide in one direction at a time, so the parts cannot be slid together without cutting them into halves or bending them. Rejected.
- **Points on the axis** (tip at the top instead of a notch): all four parts would share the top and bottom points, leaving only 18 distinct points.
- **Faceted wireframe parts** like the star ornament: the reference photo shows solid parts, and the wide slots (see below) would cut through the ribs. Solid parts are sturdier.
- **One STL per part** or rotating the parts to nest them: the webapp shows one STL per model, and a 2 × 2 grid already fits.

## Tech Specs

### Design and parameters

Default parameters (all in `models/star-24/generate.py`, millimeters):

| Parameter | Default | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| `POINTS` | 6 | points per part |
| `PARTS` | 4 | planes are `180° / PARTS` apart |
| `OUTER_RADIUS` | 45 | center to point → ~90 mm star |
| `INNER_RADIUS` | 18 | center to notch; slim points like in the photo |
| `THICKNESS` | 3.0 | |
| `TIP_ROUNDING` | 1.0 | rounds points so they don't peel or snag |
| `SLOT_CLEARANCE` | 0.3 | |
| `LOOP_OUTER` / `LOOP_INNER` | 4.0 / 2.0 | hanging loop on part A |
| `PLATE_GAP` | 5 | |
| `PLATE_COLUMNS` | 2 | 2 × 2 grid on the plate |

Geometry:

- **Outline:** `star(POINTS, OUTER_RADIUS, INNER_RADIUS)` rotated by `180° / POINTS` so a notch is at +Y, rounded by `TIP_ROUNDING`.
- **Slot width:** neighbouring parts cross at 45°, so another part's 3 mm slab covers a wider strip of a part than 3 mm. The slot width is `THICKNESS × cot(angle / 2) + SLOT_CLEARANCE` with `angle = 180° / PARTS` (≈ 7.5 mm for 4 parts). It covers every other part's slab across this part's whole thickness.
- **Segments:** the axis from `-INNER_RADIUS` to `+INNER_RADIUS` is split into `PARTS` equal segments, part A owning the top one. Part k gets a slot from its segment's top edge up through the top notch (except A) and from its segment's bottom edge down through the bottom notch (except D).
- **Hanging loop:** a ring on part A, standing on the top notch, fused to it. Part A owns the top segment, so nothing else is there; the collision test also checks it clears the neighbouring parts' upper points.
- **Printability:** plain extrusions, flat on the bed, no supports.
- **Plate:** `layout_on_plate(parts, PLATE_GAP, columns=PLATE_COLUMNS)`, `assert_printable`, `write_stl(..., "models/star-24/star-24.stl", "star-24")`.

A prototype of this geometry (default parameters) gives four connected parts, zero intersection volume for every pair when assembled, and no collision while sliding B, C and D in.

### `project.json`

```json
{
  "name": "24-point star",
  "description": "Slot-together star with 24 points: four flat six-pointed stars that slide onto each other along a common axis.\nPrint all four parts flat without supports (PLA, 0.2 mm layers). Hold part A (with the loop) upright, then push parts B, C and D up onto it from below, each turned 45° further, until they stop."
}
```

The parts are told apart by their slots: A is slotted from below only (and has the loop), D from above only, B has the shorter slot from above, C the longer one. The plate lays them out in order A, B, C, D.

### Tests (pytest)

- `tools/stlkit/tests/test_solids.py`: with `columns`, parts are laid out in rows, bounding boxes don't overlap, neighbours are `gap` apart, the whole plate is centered on the origin and sits on Z = 0; without `columns` the result is unchanged (one row).
- `models/star-24/test_generate.py`:
  - every part is printable and one connected piece; the plate fits 220 × 220 × 250 mm;
  - the assembled star has 24 distinct points (tip directions of all parts, no two closer than a few degrees);
  - **assembly check:** all four parts in their assembled pose have (close to) zero intersection volume pairwise;
  - **insertion check:** sliding B, C and D in from below along the axis (in small steps) never intersects the parts already placed;
  - slot width matches the formula, and along the axis every point between the notches belongs to exactly one part's segment;
  - the committed `star-24.stl` equals freshly generated bytes.

CI: the existing `models.yml` workflow already runs `pytest` on `tools/**` and `models/**`; no change.

## Open Questions and Assumptions

- The reference photo does not show how its parts interlock; the common-axis arrangement is chosen because it can be slid together without bending. Its look: rings of 8 points above, on and below the equator.
- 0.3 mm slot clearance (a bit more than the ornament's 0.2 mm, since each slot holds three other parts) — adjust after a test print.
- Build volume stays at the toolkit default of 220 × 220 × 250 mm.
