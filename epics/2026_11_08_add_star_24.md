## Architecture Decision

### A slotted cross and two folded parts (phase 4b)

Four six-pointed stars of two shapes, two of each, built as in the reference video:

- **A and B (cross parts):** a notch on the Y axis. A has a slit (`THICKNESS + SLOT_CLEARANCE` wide) from the bottom notch up to the center, B the same from the top notch down (B is A upside down). They slot together into a classic cross.
- **C and D (fold parts, identical):** a point on the Y axis and one closed slit through the center along it, as wide as A's slit and twice as long (`2 × INNER_RADIUS`), so it stays well inside the points.
- **Fold line:** across the slit, along the X axis through the center (through the notches). A groove from the top face (`FOLD_WIDTH` 1 mm wide, leaving `FOLD_THICKNESS` 0.6 mm) lets the part fold; thinner would risk breaking.
- **Assembly:** slot A and B into a cross; fold C in half along its fold line and slide it onto the cross through its slit. It **stays folded**. The same with D.

**Assembled (folded) state**, shown in `combined.stl`: A and B form the cross (A in the XZ plane, B in the YZ plane). C and D are folded to 60° about a hinge parallel to X, their slits lie in B's plane, and their halves split one side of A into three 60° angles: C on one side of A, D on the other. The hinge sits `HINGE_OFFSET` (1.5 mm) beside A's middle plane, which keeps the slit ends clear of B's notches. The test print checks the real fit. (An earlier version modeled C and D unfolded around a common axis, with assembly-step views; that didn't match the video and was removed.)

### Toolkit change

`layout_on_plate` currently places parts in one row. Four ~90 mm stars in a row are ~380 mm wide, more than the 220 mm plate. It gets an optional `columns` argument (default: all parts in one row, so the star ornament's STL does not change) and fills rows of `columns` parts, `gap` apart, centered on the origin.

### Alternatives Considered

- **Common-axis slide-on design** (phase 3, replaced): four parts, one segment of the axis each, slid on one after another. It slides without bending but doesn't look like the photo.
- **Tilted planes** (4a, dropped): all four parts identical, but each pair is linked through the other's center hole, so they can't be assembled.
- **Faceted wireframe parts** like the star ornament: the reference photo shows solid parts. Solid parts are sturdier.
- **One STL per part** or rotating the parts to nest them: the webapp shows one STL per model, and a 2 × 2 grid already fits.

## Tech Specs

### Design and parameters (phase 4b)

Parameters in `models/star-24/generate.py` (millimeters): `POINTS` 6, `OUTER_RADIUS` 45, `INNER_RADIUS` 18, `THICKNESS` 1.2, `TIP_ROUNDING` 1.0, `SLOT_CLEARANCE` 0.3, `FOLD_WIDTH` 1.0, `FOLD_THICKNESS` 0.6, `PLATE_GAP` 5, `PLATE_COLUMNS` 2 (2 × 2 grid).

- A, B: `star(...).rotate(30)` (notch on the Y axis), rounded, minus the slit from a notch to the center (`SLIT_LENGTH = INNER_RADIUS`).
- C, D: the unrotated star (point on the Y axis), rounded, minus the centered slit of `CENTER_SLIT_LENGTH = 2 × SLIT_LENGTH`, plus the fold groove cut from the top face, so it prints without supports.
- Printability: plain extrusions, flat on the bed. No hanging loop; hang with a thread.

### Files and `project.json`

All in `models/star-24/`, in this order: `combined.stl` "Assembled star" (viewing only, one color per part via `write_colored_stl`: A red, B blue, C green, D yellow), `star-24.stl` "Print plate" (2 × 2 grid) and `part-a.stl` … `part-d.stl`. The description says: two shapes, two of each; slot A and B into a cross; fold C in half along its thin line and slide it onto the cross through its slit, where it stays folded; repeat with D; hang with a thread.

### Tests (pytest)

- `tools/stlkit/tests/test_solids.py`: grid layout (phase 2).
- `models/star-24/test_generate.py`:
  - parts are printable and one piece; the plate fits 220 × 220 × 250 mm;
  - two shapes: B is A rotated by 180°, C and D are identical;
  - A's slit runs from the notch to the center; C's slit is as wide as A's, twice as long, and closed at both ends;
  - the groove leaves `FOLD_THICKNESS` (≥ 0.6 mm) on the fold line and `THICKNESS` beside it; A and B have no groove;
  - assembled: no two parts intersect; C's and D's halves make three 60° angles with A on opposite sides; B passes through their slits;
  - the assembled view has one color per part;
  - `project.json` lists exactly the generated files, in order;
  - every committed STL equals freshly generated bytes.

CI: the existing `models.yml` workflow already runs `pytest` on `tools/**` and `models/**`; no change.
